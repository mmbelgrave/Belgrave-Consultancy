Where I can help

Does this sound like your company?

Most companies do not fail for lack of a strategy. They get stuck in the gaps: between vision and budget, between board and management, between management and the people running projects. Six situations I am asked about most.

Growth

We have outgrown our structure.

  • Everything still runs through you, or the same two or three people.
  • Nobody is quite sure who decides what, so decisions queue and meetings end without a conclusion.
  • What worked at 40 people creaks at 120. New managers do not know what they own.
  • The budget says one thing, the capacity of your people another.

What helps: roles, decision rights and a management rhythm that fit the company you are now. Organisation design

Too large

The organisation is bigger than the business needs.

  • You inherited a structure sized for another order book, owner or market.
  • Overhead is heavy against today’s revenue and backlog, but nobody agrees what can go.
  • You fear the best people leave first, and that knowledge walks out with them.
  • Those who stay are anxious, and the messages they hear differ.

What helps: a capacity review against budget and backlog, and a change plan that protects the capability you must keep. Organisation design

Transition

A new direction, leadership or owner.

  • A new owner or managing director, and a team still finding its footing.
  • The strategy is on slides but has not reached budgets, roles or projects.
  • The board wants decision-ready numbers; management has spreadsheets and anecdotes.
  • You need a sparring partner who is neither employee nor auditor.

What helps: a strategy translated into plan, budget and organisation, with coaching alongside the work. Strategy · Coaching

Delivery

Projects slip, and the numbers arrive too late.

  • Forecasts are always a little behind reality.
  • Margin leaks away through changes nobody priced.
  • Problems surface at the month-end or at project close, and nobody owns the early warning.
  • Each department reports a different version of the same project.

What helps: forecasting discipline, early warning and reporting people can act on. Project control

Systems

A system decision is coming, and the business is not ready.

  • Every department has its own spreadsheet and nobody has a single source of truth.
  • The vendor demo looked perfect and the quote looks reasonable.
  • Leadership agrees you need a new system, but not on how the business actually works.
  • You have been through one before and it was harder than it should have been.

What helps: preparation from the business side, before the vendor is in the room. ERP support · The ERP guide

AI

Everyone says we should use AI. Nobody can say where it would pay.

  • Competitors and clients ask what you are doing with AI.
  • Pilots run and never reach daily work.
  • Too much knowledge sits with a few experts, and you worry about confidentiality.
  • You suspect the operating model has to change, but not how.

What helps: starting from your business problems and processes, not from the tools. AI and the operating model

Selected experience

What the work looks like in practice.

Offshore wind and energy engineering · KCI Offshore Engineering, part of Sif Group

Senior strategic advisor to the management team

Context

A specialist engineering company in a changing market, with new management, a recovery agenda and a strategy for its next phase to define.

My role

Working directly with management and the group board, and as sparring partner to the managing director and operations director. Coaching newly appointed managers.

The work
  • Strategy and organisation strategy for the next phase
  • Business plan, budget and capacity review linked to the backlog outlook
  • Management and role structure, including product-line ownership
  • Project control, governance and board reporting
  • Pricing and partnership models, and a proposal framework
  • ERP and document-management selection and implementation
What was delivered
  • A redefined strategy and organisation, with a new business plan and departmental plans
  • Clear mandates and a redesigned change process and authorisation matrix
  • Reporting to management and board
  • Standard proposal templates and commercial models

I publish measurable results only when the client has agreed to them.
