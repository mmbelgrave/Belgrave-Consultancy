Does this sound familiar?

Most ERP projects are decided before they start.

The decision to implement an ERP rarely arrives as a grand strategic moment. It creeps up on you, until the business is steered on information that is always slightly behind reality. Then the project begins, and the same problems come back in a new form.

What you may be living withWhat the guide gives you
“Reporting that is always slightly behind reality, and every department with its own spreadsheet.”A clear case for change, and objectives that say what success looks like before anything is bought.Chapters 1 and 4
“A vendor demo that looked perfect, and a quote that looks reasonable.”A way to choose on weighted criteria and three-year total cost, not on who impressed you in a meeting room.Chapter 5, Appendix C
“A leadership team that agrees you need a new system, but not on how the business actually works.”Process mapping that gets the whole team to agree how you operate, and a readiness score before you commit.Chapters 2 and 3, Appendices A and B
“A requirements phase planned for four weeks that is heading for ten, and a budget that keeps growing.”Governance, a scope that stays still, requirements the business agrees on, and a budget that includes internal time and contingency.Chapters 4, 6, 7 and 8
“A go-live that was meant to transform the business and delivers months of chaos.”Data migration, testing and change management planned in from the start, a go/no-go checklist, and a plan for the first 90 days.Chapters 9 to 12, Appendices E and F

The software was never the hard part. The hard part is knowing your own business clearly enough to tell the software what to do. That work is almost entirely under your control, and it is what this guide is about.

Is this guide about a specific ERP system?

No. It is deliberately vendor-neutral and applies to any ERP, or any business-critical system that touches several departments.

My business is smaller than 30 people, or larger than 250. Is it still useful?

The principles apply at any size. The examples and team structures are written for growing businesses of roughly 30 to 250 people.

We are already mid-implementation. Is it too late?

No, the principles apply at every stage. But if you need help with a live project now, a conversation with me will be more useful than the guide alone.

How long does it take to read?

The leadership boxes take under an hour. The full guide is designed to be read once and then used as a reference throughout the project.
